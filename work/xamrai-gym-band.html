Xamrai Gym Band

A minimalist wearable that strips the digital noise out of fitness tracking. Turn a dial, feel the click, get back to the bar.

Category
Wearable · Fitness
Scope
Industrial design, mechanism design, prototyping, DfM
Mechanism
Custom internal gear train, indexed dials, detent spring
Manufacture
High-grade 3D printing
Power
None. Manual input only.
(01) The problem

Your phone is the most distracting thing in the gym.

Every workout tracker wants to be a computer: a screen to wake, a battery to charge, an app to sync, a notification waiting the moment you look down. For athletes who train for focus as much as for strength, that is the opposite of what the moment needs.

The brief we set ourselves was simple to say and hard to build: track sets and reps reliably, survive sweat and knocks, and ask for nothing in return — no charging, no pairing, no signal.

Power required0 V

Every count is stored mechanically — in the position of a gear, not in memory.

(02) The mechanism

A counter you can feel with your eyes closed.

At the heart of the band is a custom-engineered internal gear system driven entirely by manual input. Two precision-tuned dials — one for sets, one for reps — advance in crisp, indexed steps, so each count lands with a distinct click you can feel through a glove.

Getting that click right was most of the work. Too soft and you lose count mid-set; too stiff and it becomes a chore. We iterated the detent geometry and spring through printed test coupons until the action was fast, positive and consistent across the whole dial.

(a) Durable

Built for chalk and sweat

A sealed, sweat-resistant housing and a strap made to be wiped down, dropped, and worn every day.

(b) Tactile

Precision-tuned dials

Knurled edges and indexed detents give unmistakable feedback between sets — no looking, no tapping.

(c) Sustainable

Printed, not tooled

High-grade 3D printing keeps production lean: no expensive moulds, small batches, and parts made only when needed.

(03) Designing for the printer

Manufacturing wasn’t the last step. It was the first constraint.

Because the band is produced additively, every part was designed around how it prints: orientation chosen for strength along the load path, clearances tuned for the process instead of copied from an injection-moulding rulebook, and features sized so the gear teeth come off the printer ready to run.

The result is a product that can be made in small runs without a factory retooling — which is exactly what a focused, independent fitness product needs on its way to market.
