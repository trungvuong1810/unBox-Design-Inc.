(Why “unBox”)

Too many good ideas stay in the box — stuck as a sketch, a rendering, a prototype nobody can afford to make.

The name is a promise. We take products out of the concept phase and into the world, and we do it by being as comfortable with gate locations and print orientations as we are with proportion and surface.

It’s also a reminder of where products end up: in someone’s hands, coming out of a box for the first time. That moment should feel considered — and it can only happen if the thing can actually be made.

(Founder)

Trung Vuong

Trung is an industrial designer who founded unBox to bridge the gap between creative industrial design and the technical rigour of the factory floor. His work spans wearables, medical equipment and lighting — products that look nothing alike, but share an obsession with how they will be made.

He is happiest somewhere between the sketchbook and the 3D printer: shaping a surface in CAD in the morning, testing a printed mechanism by hand in the afternoon, and rewriting the design that evening based on what the part had to say.

Clients work with Trung directly, from the first conversation to the first production sample.

(Principles)

What we believe, in four flaps

Beautiful and buildable.

The best designs aren’t just beautiful; they are manufacturable. We refuse to treat those as competing goals.

Integrity at every step.

Ideas tend to get diluted on their way to production. We protect the original intent from napkin sketch to first article.

Prove it in hand.

Renderings flatter. Prototypes tell the truth. We build early and often, and let the objects make the argument.

Fewer, better parts.

Every part removed is a cost saved, a failure avoided and an assembly simplified. Restraint is a manufacturing strategy.
