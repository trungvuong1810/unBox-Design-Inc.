How a product unfolds

Five phases, one continuous line of thought. Manufacturing isn’t a phase at the end — it is a question we ask from the very first sketch.

Typical length
Weeks for a focused DfM pass · months for a new product
Check-ins
Weekly, with a review at the end of each phase
Flexibility
Join at any phase — many clients arrive mid-way
Phase
01

Unpack

Every project starts by taking the idea out of its box and laying everything on the table: who it is for, what it must do, what it must cost, and where it might be made. We review existing products, talk to users where we can, and turn assumptions into a brief with clear, testable requirements.

The output is alignment — a shared understanding of the problem that keeps every later decision honest.

Deliverables
  • Product brief
  • Requirements list
  • Competitive review
  • Target cost & volume
Phase
02

Shape

Pencils first. We explore broadly through sketches, quick printed form studies and rough mock-ups, then narrow to two or three directions worth developing. Each direction is presented with how it would be made, not just how it would look.

You choose a direction knowing its trade-offs — and nothing beautiful is chosen that can’t be built.

Deliverables
  • Sketch boards
  • Form studies
  • Concept renderings
  • Direction review
Phase
03

Prove

The chosen concept moves into detailed 3D CAD and into your hands. We build functional prototypes — mechanisms, fits, seals, ergonomics — and test them against the brief. When something fails, we would rather it fail here, on a printer, than later, in a mould.

This is where clicks are tuned, wall thicknesses argued over, and assumptions replaced with evidence.

Deliverables
  • Detailed CAD
  • Works-like prototypes
  • Looks-like model
  • Test findings
Phase
04

Refine

Design for manufacturing in earnest. We optimise every part for its process — draft, walls, ribs and parting lines for moulding; orientation, supports and clearances for printing — consolidate parts where we can, and simplify the assembly so it goes together the same way every time.

The goal: lower cost and lower risk, with the design’s character intact.

Deliverables
  • Production CAD
  • Drawings & tolerances
  • CMF specification
  • Bill of materials
Phase
05

Ship

We prepare a supplier-ready package and stay involved as production starts, whether you are manufacturing in Ontario or overseas. We answer supplier questions, review samples and first articles, and resolve the small surprises that every first run brings.

We consider a project finished when good parts are coming off the line — not when the last file is emailed.

Deliverables
  • Supplier package
  • Print / tooling files
  • Sample reviews
  • Production fixes
(Questions)

Frequently asked

Do you sign NDAs?

Yes, happily and routinely — before you share anything sensitive. Much of our contract work is confidential by default.

Who owns the design and the files?

You do. On payment, the intellectual property in the work we create for you, and all CAD and production files, transfer to you.

How is a project priced?

New product development is quoted per phase, so you commit one step at a time. Contract design is usually billed hourly or as a fixed block of time. After a first conversation we send a written proposal with scope, timeline and cost.

Can you help if I already have a design?

Yes — that is often where we are most useful. We can review an existing design, rebuild messy CAD, run a design-for-manufacturing pass, or prototype what you have so far.

Do you work with manufacturers overseas?

Yes. We prepare documentation that suppliers can quote and build from wherever they are, and support you through sample reviews and first production runs, locally or abroad.

Do you only work with clients in Toronto?

No. We are based in Toronto and can meet in person across the GTA, but most collaboration happens remotely, and we work with clients anywhere.
